Buying guide

How to choose an outdoor jacket

A step-by-step approach to finding a jacket that suits your climate, your activities and your style, without paying for features you will never use.

Step 1

Start with how you’ll use it

Before looking at features, think honestly about where you will wear the jacket most. A commuter in a rainy city, a weekend hiker and someone walking a dog across muddy fields all need different things.

  • Daily commuting: water resistance, a hood that does not block side vision, and a clean look.
  • Hiking: waterproofing, breathability, low weight and a hood that fits over a hat.
  • Countryside and dog walking: durable fabric that shrugs off thorns and mud, with big pockets.
  • Cold, still conditions: insulation first, with a windproof outer layer.
Step 2

Match jacket type to conditions

Step 3

Details that make a difference

  • Hood: adjustable at the back and sides so it turns with your head; a stiffened peak keeps rain off your face.
  • Zip and storm flap: a flap or water-resistant zip stops wind and rain getting through the front.
  • Cuffs and hem: adjustable tabs and drawcords seal out drafts.
  • Pockets: hand-warmer pockets placed above a backpack hip belt are useful for hikers.
  • Venting: underarm zips release heat during uphill effort.
  • Seams: taped or welded seams are essential on fully waterproof jackets.
Step 4

Think long term

The most sustainable jacket is the one you keep wearing. Choose a style you genuinely like, in a colour that works with most of your wardrobe, and favour designs that can be repaired: replaceable zips, re-waxable cotton, and re-proofable shells.

Look for clear care instructions and, where possible, information about how and where the jacket was made. A slightly higher upfront cost often means many more years of use.
