Care guide

Jacket care, fabric by fabric

Cleaning, re-proofing, storing and small repairs. A little attention once or twice a season keeps outerwear performing and looking good for years.

Waterproof shells

Wash it, don’t fear it

Dirt and body oils clog breathable membranes and make the water-repellent finish fail. Washing a shell actually helps it perform better.

  1. Prepare itClose all zips and Velcro and empty the pockets.

  2. Use a technical washChoose one made for outdoor gear; regular detergent and softener leave residues.

  3. Rinse thoroughlyAdd an extra rinse cycle if possible.

  4. Reactivate the finishUse low heat in a dryer or a cool iron, only if the care label allows it.

  5. Re-proof when neededIf water stops beading on the surface, apply a spray-on or wash-in proofer.

Waxed cotton

Sponge with cold water only. Re-wax once or twice a year, or whenever the fabric looks pale and dry at the seams and shoulders.

Down jackets

Wash rarely with a down-specific cleaner. Dry slowly on low heat with clean tennis or dryer balls to break up clumps.

Wool coats

Brush after wearing, air it out, and spot-clean. Leave deep cleaning to a specialist cleaner.

Storage

Between seasons

  • Always clean a jacket before storing it; stains and oils set over time.
  • Make sure it is completely dry to prevent mildew, especially waxed cotton.
  • Hang structured jackets on broad, shaped hangers to protect the shoulders.
  • Store down jackets loosely, never compressed in a stuff sack for months.
  • Use breathable cotton garment bags rather than plastic.
Small repairs

Fix it early

Most jacket problems start small. A stuck zip, a loose button or a tiny tear is easy to fix if you deal with it promptly.

  • Sticky zips: clean the teeth with a soft brush and use a zip lubricant or a little wax.
  • Small tears in shells: seal with a purpose-made repair patch on clean, dry fabric.
  • Leather scuffs: wipe clean and apply a suitable leather conditioner sparingly after a spot test.
  • Bigger jobs: replaced zips, re-taped seams or relined coats are best handled by a repair specialist.

Always follow the care label on your jacket first.
